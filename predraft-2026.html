The State of the League

Welcome to Year Two. The Hoe Ass Dynasty League's first rookie draft is one week away, and the league has already done the thing every dynasty league eventually does: separated itself into tiers based on who actually pays attention. The defending champ is positioned to reload without panic. The runner-up is aging in dog years. A handful of middle-class contenders are jockeying for position with a full allotment of picks. And at the bottom, there's a rebuild that may or may not be a rebuild, depending on which day you ask Terra. The 2026 rookie class skews heavy at the top with elite WR talent and a couple of high-upside RBs led by Jeremiyah Love, who is essentially already drafted at 1.01. Everything else is up for grabs, and so is everyone's patience.

Power Rankings

  1. Can't Kill Bill 🗡️ — Quietly walked out of last year's startup with an excellent young core, and then proceeded to add Bucky Irving in what may end up being the heist of the decade. The Amon-Ra/London/Rice receiver room anchors a roster with an average age that flirts with college eligibility, and the only real "weakness" is at tight end. The fact that he's now openly shopping 1.03 from a position of strength is a savvy move.
  2. My team REEKS — Missing the playoffs last year clearly did not result in a roster teardown, because the roster never needed one. The Puka-Bowers spine is generational, the WR depth is enviable, and the 2027 first acquired in "The Trade" hangs over this league like a storm cloud. Somehow has a full slate of picks and almost zero pressure to use them. The competitive window is wide open and the manager knows it.
  3. The Tet Offensive — Defending champ with a Bijan-sized engine and a Bijan-sized problem behind him at RB2, because Allgeier just got Allgeier'd by Jeremiyah Love in Arizona. The early third-rounder from Terra is the kind of small edge a co-commissioner accumulates without anyone really noticing.
  4. BatemanS — Stacked at tight end to the point of inefficiency, which is why the pre-draft chatter has him dangling one. Ja'Marr Chase and Jonathan Taylor headline a roster that doesn't have many holes beyond WR depth. The WR room behind Chase and Waddle is where the draft has to make an impact.
  5. kdicillo — Last season was a medical chart, not a fantasy season. The roster underneath the injury luck is legitimately solid: Nabers, Judkins, Garrett Wilson, Lamar, with no glaring holes to plug. This is the rare team that can genuinely take BPA without flinching.
  6. Green Eggs and Hampton — Eight running backs deep. The Gibbs/Hampton/Henderson trio is genuinely terrifying and might be the league's best RB room, but the WR depth chart drops off a cliff after Pickens. The vibes-based drafting strategy somehow produced a playoff team last year, and the temptation to grab another running back in the first round will be real even if the roster is begging for receivers.
  7. SomethingStrange 4 Change — A 2-seed last season that could use both a QB and a TE upgrade going into this draft. The CeeDee/Olave/McConkey trio is impressive, the RB room is functional, and the holes are surgical rather than systemic. Locked-in manager, defined needs, four picks to work with — a clean draft setup.
  8. 11dhall — The win-now roster that fell short in the championship game last season has aged exactly as predicted, which is to say poorly. McCaffrey is twenty-nine, Adams is thirty-three, Derrick Henry is on someone else's roster but the energy is the same. The roster age is a flashing warning light, but the manager does not check the dashboard. Whatever happens here is going to happen on autopilot.
  9. Ms. New Boutte — Already flipped Stafford and Gainwell to Terra for a 2028 first, which is the kind of patient move that usually pays off. Jeanty is the cornerstone, the WR room has upside swings in Burden and Hunter, and the draft approach will almost certainly involve more picks by the time it ends than at the start.
  10. A Swift End — Justin Jefferson and Trey McBride are the two pillars holding up a roster that has been actively dismantling itself in real time. The Bucky-for-a-second trade is now folklore. Recent offseason moves suggest a manager still figuring out what kind of team he's building. Has more picks than anyone in the league, which is an opportunity to right the ship.
  11. bhstevens21 — The win-now bet from year one didn't pay, and now the roster is older without being elite. Achane is the centerpiece, Saquon and Henry are aging assets pretending to still be cornerstones, and the receiver room is a mess. The fork in the road is here, and the draft strategy will tell us which direction.
  12. julianad112 — The Brock Bowers trade is going to age like milk for the next four years and there's nothing to be done about it. Jeremiyah Love at the 1.01 helps. The roster also still has Harrison Jr., Higgins, and Jacobs, which makes it less of a true rebuild and more of a "what are we doing exactly" situation.

Storylines to Watch

Does jaytherulerx21 actually move the 1.03? He's announced his intentions in the group chat, which is either a real signal or pre-draft theater. Trading down from 1.03 in a top-heavy class is the right move if a TE he likes falls; it's a disaster if he overvalues quantity over the rookie class's actual shape.

What does Terra do at 1.02? Carnell Tate is the consensus pick. But Terra has a Bucky Irving-sized hole in his RB room, which leads some to believe Jadarian Price might be the pick at 1.02. Five picks should be a launching pad for a rebuild. Used wrong, it's a five-car pileup.

rdfergus is sitting on a 2027 first that nobody else has. That pick is the most valuable trade chip in the league, and it isn't even being used this draft. Watch for him to leverage it sideways — package a 2026 second plus a sweetener for someone's 2026 first, and suddenly the roster gets even more obnoxious.

The Bateman TE fire sale. Loveland, Pitts, and a deep room means at least one of those guys is on the block. The asking price will tell us a lot about how the rest of the league values tight ends in this format.

The dhall autopick clock. Last year's runner-up has a roster begging for youth and a manager who reportedly doesn't have notifications on. The picks are going to be made one way or another. Whether they're made by him is the question.
